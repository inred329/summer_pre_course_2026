# Constitution of the 2026 Summer Preparatory Course

Version: 2.0.0-draft  
Status: Refactoring draft; does not yet replace the official 1.3.0 baseline  
Primary audience: Course designers, instructors, and maintainers

## Preamble

This Constitution defines the course's non-negotiable core values and governance principles.

It does not regulate details that may change with tools, platforms, material formats, or teaching practice. Such details shall be defined by Standards, Guides, and official policy documents.

# Part I — Core Purpose

## Article 1 — Highest Purpose

Every course design, material, teaching activity, assessment, and use of tools shall serve one highest purpose: helping students build correct, verifiable, and transferable understanding and capability.

Student safety, dignity, and fairness are non-negotiable. Student understanding and technical correctness must not be traded against or sacrificed for one another.

## Article 2 — Scope and Principles of Adjudication

This Constitution applies to all official course designs, documents, materials, teaching activities, assessments, and maintenance work.

When principles conflict, decisions shall follow these rules:

1. Student safety, dignity, and fairness must not be sacrificed.
2. Readability must not be used to lower technical correctness, and technical completeness must not be used to create unnecessary barriers to understanding.
3. The Chinese-taught and English-taught classes shall remain substantively equivalent.
4. Documentation clarity, traceability, and maintainability take precedence over teaching convenience, document output, and product completion.

# Part II — Document Governance

## Article 3 — Document Levels and Authoritative Sources

Course documents are organized into four governance levels:

1. Constitution: defines non-negotiable and long-term stable principles.
2. Standards: define consistency requirements, templates, formats, and quality baselines.
3. Guides and Policies: define adaptable execution methods, processes, and current-term rules.
4. Materials: provide actual learning and teaching content for students or instructors.

Each official policy or rule shall have one authoritative source. Other documents may summarize, explain, or link to it for their readers, but must not redefine it independently and create conflicting versions.

## Article 4 — Primary Audience and Document Boundaries

Every official document shall have one primary audience and shall prioritize that audience.

Governance and design documents shall be precise and traceable; instructor documents shall be executable and adaptable; student materials shall be fluent, coherent, and suitable for continuous reading; review documents shall judge current work through explicit criteria and evidence and shall not create new rules by themselves.

Internal governance information must not pollute the main student reading flow. Every official document shall be reasonably discoverable and reachable by its primary audience.

## Article 5 — Bilingual Completion and Substantive Equivalence

Every official document shall be available in Traditional Chinese and English and shall preserve the same purpose, core content, requirements, code, test data, assessment conditions, and version status.

Translation may adapt sentence structure, pacing, and explanation style to the target language, but must not change substantive requirements. Both language versions shall be updated within the same unit of work.

# Part III — Student Learning and Materials

## Article 6 — Student Right to Readable Materials

Students have the right to materials written in natural language, with coherent paragraphs, clear structure, and technical correctness.

Student materials shall build understanding through necessary explanation, reasoning, examples, and verification. Specification fields, internal IDs, instructor notes, maintenance procedures, or excessive lists must not replace these elements.

When information density, shorter length, or maintenance convenience obstructs understanding, the course shall protect students' ability to continue reading and build understanding without removing necessary constraints, technical conditions, or important boundaries.

## Article 7 — Teaching and Learning Principles

The course shall establish a problem context, concept, and mental model before introducing syntax and tools.

Important learning activities shall allow students to form expectations, implement or reason, observe results, diagnose errors, modify, and verify. A single correct output, passed test, or completed product is insufficient evidence of full understanding.

A new concept must not depend on required knowledge that has not yet been established. When the learning objective does not require one specific technique, multiple reasonable solutions shall be allowed.

# Part IV — Technical Correctness, AI, and Assessment

## Article 8 — Technical Correctness and Verifiability

Official code, diagrams, tests, input/output, error classifications, and explanatory text must be mutually consistent and verified against the specified standard, target environment, and reproducible evidence.

Testing and debugging shall be evidence-based. Automated validation may support technical correctness, but it does not replace human review of pedagogy and readability.

## Article 9 — AI and External Tools

AI and other tools may support learning but must not replace students in requirement understanding, initial reasoning, testing judgment, debugging decisions, result verification, or oral explanation.

AI use is optional by default; not using AI is not evidence of missing capability. AI responses are not authoritative evidence and must be verified through programs, tests, compiler diagnostics, reliable sources, or other reproducible methods before adoption.

Authors and maintainers remain fully responsible when AI is used to create or modify official content.

## Article 10 — Assessment and Capability Evidence

Assessment shall correspond to explicit capabilities and shall use enough diverse evidence to support a judgment.

Successful compilation, correct output, passing an Online Judge, submitting a complete program, AI approval, or reciting a definition cannot individually prove full capability.

Before formal assessment, students shall know its purpose, form, primary capabilities, judgment criteria, and permitted resources. Detailed percentages and current-term procedures shall be defined by the official bilingual assessment policy.

# Part V — Equivalence, Maintenance, and Amendment

## Article 11 — Substantive Equivalence Between Language Tracks

The Chinese-taught and English-taught classes shall achieve the same core capabilities and technical depth.

Pacing, number of examples, language support, and interaction methods may differ, but core content and assessment standards must not be reduced because of the teaching language. Language fluency must not replace programming capability in assessment.

## Article 12 — Maintenance and Amendment

Major changes shall explain their rationale, impact, and affected authoritative sources, and shall provide synchronized Chinese and English versions with a reviewable change record.

The Constitution shall contain only non-negotiable and long-term stable principles. Every added or retained article must identify the core value it protects and confirm that Standards, Guides, or official policy documents cannot adequately govern the matter.

As the course matures, this Constitution should become clearer and shorter rather than expand whenever a new problem appears.
