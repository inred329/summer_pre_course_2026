## Historical Purpose

The original maturity review used experience from bilingual Units, instructor resources, assessment documents, and technical validation to identify which rules should remain constitutional principles and which details should move into lower-level standards, templates, or operating procedures.

Its central recommendation was to replace Constitution 1.3.0 with a shorter, principle-based Constitution 2.0 and to remove outdated universal AI logging/assessment obligations.

## Historical Recommendations That Were Carried Forward

The following themes materially informed Constitution 2.0 and the current repository structure:

- student understanding, safety, dignity, fairness, and capability growth take priority;
- technical correctness and reproducible evidence are non-negotiable;
- concepts precede syntax;
- prediction, testing, debugging, modification, and verification are legitimate learning evidence;
- Chinese and English core content must remain substantively equivalent;
- student, instructor, and maintainer documents need distinct primary audiences;
- the root README should route readers instead of duplicating policy;
- assessment policy should have a single authoritative source;
- AI/external assistance should not replace thinking and should not become a universal completion or assessment requirement;
- detailed writing templates and maintenance procedures belong below the Constitution.

## Historical Recommendations That Must Not Be Read as Current Policy

The original document discussed then-current article numbers, draft replacement structures, possible supporting standards, and specific migration actions. Those proposals are now superseded by the actual current Constitution 2.0, governance index, assessment policy, design standards, and active audit.

Do not use this file to infer current:

- Constitution article wording;
- mandatory document structures;
- assessment percentages or procedures;
- AI/tool-use requirements;
- required supporting-document names;
- repository next steps.
